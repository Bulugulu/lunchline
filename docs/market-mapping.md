# Part 2: AI-Enabled Market Mapping and Sourcing Strategy

## Status: Direction locked (2026-05-31) — structuring the deliverable; vertical to be selected *by* the methodology, not assumed

## Locked direction (from 2026-05-31 brainstorming with Aviv)

1. **End-market family = knowledge-services, ON the case's approved list** (not a propose-your-own). Keeps us inside the spirit of the brief while letting Aviv bring credible original perspective.
2. **Specific market = boutique RETAINED executive search.** Higher-margin, relationship/judgment moat that is *more* defensible against AI than contingent/volume staffing; classic owner-dependent, succession-driven sellers; thousands of sub-$50M owner-operated boutiques. Niche staffing was considered and rejected (more commoditized, thinner margins, more AI-exposed).
3. **Single-vertical anchor, NOT horizontal.** Specificity beats breadth for the case's "clear, defended POV." Industry knowledge + relationships are the moat, so anchor where domain expertise is most valuable.
4. **Geographic anchor = East Coast / Northeast corridor.** Aviv's call: focus where there is a *local presence* so the market map and sourcing funnel are geographically tight. This mirrors the real search-fund reality ("operate where you are") and is itself an original framing vs. a generic national map.
5. **AI/ML-talent vertical explicitly REJECTED** — crowded "fad" market, fuzzy skill definitions, young founders (weak succession-seller supply).
6. **The vertical is NOT pre-assumed.** Per Aviv: "we shouldn't assume a vertical but include in our project plan an evaluation across these sectors." The *first workstream of the AI sourcing methodology IS the vertical down-select.* This converts the case constraint ("pick one market") into the thesis: *I didn't assume the market; I used the AI toolkit to choose it.* That is exactly the "synthesize messy inputs into a decision" the evaluators reward.

## Structural approach (Approach A — pending final confirmation)

- **Phase 1 = AI-driven vertical down-select** across a shortlist of legacy, Northeast-concentrated knowledge-services verticals. Candidate shortlist to screen:
  - Healthcare / Life-Sciences (Boston-Cambridge biotech + NJ/Philadelphia pharma + NYC/Northeast health systems)
  - Education / EdTech (Boston hub + NYC)
  - Legal / Professional services (NYC + DC + Boston)
  - Financial services (NYC) — included so the screen is not rigged toward Aviv's edge
  - **Scoring criteria:** fragmentation, succession-seller supply, AI-resistance of the senior tier, data availability for AI sourcing, local target density, fee economics, and operator-edge fit.
  - Output: a recommended vertical, grounded in a lightweight real research pass (evidence, not assertion).
- **Phases 2+** (deep market map, vendor stack, human-vs-automation map) are run concretely against the winning vertical. The vendor stack and automation map are largely vertical-agnostic, so they instantiate cleanly once the vertical is chosen.

Alternatives considered: **B** — map all verticals in parallel, pick at the end (more thorough but diffuse, muddies the POV). **C** — vertical-agnostic methodology with vertical choice as an appendix (too abstract; the case says "pick one"). Both rejected in favor of A.
