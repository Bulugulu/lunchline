# AENT Peer Selection Rationale

**Date:** 2026-05-29 · **Subject EV (rebuilt):** ~$389M · **TTM rev $1,108.7M · TTM Adj EBITDA $47.8M**

AENT has no clean public twin: it is simultaneously (a) a thin-margin broadline **distributor** of physical product, (b) a **physical-media** business (vinyl/CD/DVD) with a secular-decline reputation, and (c) an aspiring **owned-brand / collectibles / authentication** platform. The peer set deliberately brackets all three economic identities so the multiple debate is explicit rather than assumed.

## The structural-economics anchor (most weight)
- **ScanSource (SCSC)** — `primary`. A pure broadline distributor with a ~4.65% adjusted-EBITDA margin, essentially identical to AENT's ~4.3% TTM. It trades **7.8x EV/EBITDA / ~0.32x revenue**. This is the single most important comp because it answers the bear's core claim directly: *the market does NOT assign cheap multiples to low-margin distributors per se* — SCSC is a profitable, growing-modestly distributor and still gets ~8x EBITDA. AENT at ~8.1x EBITDA is already **in line** with the distributor anchor on EBITDA, and *cheaper* on revenue only because its margin is thinner. The 0.35x EV/revenue is therefore NOT evidence of mispricing on its own — it is the correct output of a thin gross margin. The mispricing case, if any, must come from the *mix/margin trajectory*, not the headline EV/sales.

## The collectibles-reality anchors (discipline against the bull)
- **Funko (FNKO)** — `primary`. The closest collectibles + physical-product + leverage analog. Levered (2.4x), flat sales, depressed: **~4.6x fwd EV/EBITDA, 0.54x revenue.** Funko is the cautionary comp — "collectibles" did NOT earn it a premium multiple once growth stalled and leverage bit. It disciplines the bull's "we're a collectibles company now" reframing: collectibles done at low margin with leverage trades at distributor multiples or worse.
- **GameStop (GME)** — `secondary / excluded from multiple-setting`. Same collectibles/trading-card narrative pivot, but its EV is hopelessly distorted by a ~$4.6B meme-driven cash hoard and a $9.7B market cap on $3.6B revenue. 22x EV/EBITDA is not a usable anchor; included only to show the collectibles narrative is industry-wide, not AENT-specific.

## The "owned-brand done right" aspiration anchor
- **Build-A-Bear (BBW)** — `primary`. A profitable, low-debt, licensed/owned-brand collectible-experience retailer at **10.8x EV/EBITDA / 1.7x revenue, ~15% EBITDA margin.** This is where AENT's Handmade-by-Robots / Alliance Authentic / Endstate strategy *points* if it works — owned brand + licensing + higher margin. It frames the bull's terminal: AENT does not need to become BBW, but every point of mix it shifts from pass-through distribution toward owned/authenticated product pulls its blended margin (and deserved multiple) toward the BBW end of the band.

## Excluded / prior-set notes
- **LYV (Live Nation)** and **SGA (Saga Communications)** from the prior auto-generated benchmark file are **excluded**: LYV is live-events/ticketing (28x EBITDA, totally different model — a GICS-sector artifact, since AENT is mis-tagged "Communication Services / Entertainment"), and SGA is radio broadcasting. Neither is a distributor or a physical-product business; they were sector-screen noise. The GICS "Communication Services" tag is itself the misclassification that puts AENT in the wrong comp screen.

## Multiple conclusion used in the model
- **Base distribution multiple: ~7-8x EBITDA (SCSC-anchored).** AENT is *not* mispriced on a pure-distributor basis.
- **The variant, if real, is mix:** as owned-brand/collectibles/authentication rises, blended margin and the deserved multiple drift toward the BBW band (10-11x). The bull case is a *re-rate on margin mix*, sanity-checked against FNKO as the floor (collectibles ≠ automatic premium).
