# HOUR (Hour Loop) — Peer selection rationale

**Date:** 2026-05-30 · **For:** the SOTP/relative-value legs of `model.md`

## What HOUR actually is (so the comp logic is right)
Hour Loop is **not** an Amazon aggregator (it doesn't buy/roll-up FBA brands), **not** a marketplace operator, and **not** a SaaS/e-commerce enabler. It is a **wholesale third-party reseller**: it buys other brands' goods at wholesale and resells them, ~98% of revenue **through the Amazon platform** (FY25 10-K, [item 1](../../edgar/hour/filings/2026-03-24_10-K_0001493152-26-012278.htm)). Amazon is simultaneously its **storefront, its fulfillment provider (FBA), and its biggest competitor** ("Amazon Retail … sells at a loss", "Sold by Amazon"). So the right comp universe is **small/micro-cap pure-play online retailers**, valued on **EV/Sales** (everyone in this band is at or near breakeven, so EV/EBITDA is mostly N/M).

## The single most important comp finding
Every relevant online-retail micro-cap trades at a **deeply depressed EV/Sales** *and* is **unprofitable**:
- **PETS** 0.11x EV/Sales, EBITDA −$28.8M, Altman-Z 1.6
- **PRTS** 0.11x EV/Sales, EBITDA −$13.6M, Altman-Z 0.72
- **FLWS** 0.32x EV/Sales, EBITDA −$17M, op margin −4.5%

**Caveat on "is HOUR rich vs these?" (lead Push 3):** do NOT say "HOUR at 0.46x is rich vs PETS/PRTS 0.11x." That is unfair — those peers are *unprofitable*, and a profitable reseller can legitimately carry a higher EV/Sales. The fair, narrower point is: **EV/Sales is the wrong lens for a 1.7%-margin business.** The cohort shows online resellers all print sub-0.5x EV/Sales because they live on thin margins and carry inventory — so 0.46x is unremarkable and is **not** a "deep-value" signal. The metric that matters for a thin-margin business is **EV/EBIT, on which HOUR is ~28× — an expensive, grower's multiple on a no-moat, no-growth-value reseller.** That, not the peer EV/Sales spread, is where the overvaluation shows. HOUR is distinguished from the cohort only by being **barely profitable** (FY25 op margin +1.7%, net income +$1.7M).

## Inclusion decisions
| Ticker | Inclusion | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **PETS** | primary | Closest on size + pure-online-retail + micro-cap; the profitability/sales-multiple anchor. |
| **PRTS** | primary | Pure online reseller of third-party goods; confirms the sector-wide sub-0.15x EV/Sales floor for a struggling online retailer. |
| **FLWS** | primary | Branded online retailer; ceiling case (0.32x) for an online retailer the market has soured on. |
| AMZN | excluded | HOUR's platform/supplier/competitor, not a peer; mega-cap multiples irrelevant. |
| QNST | excluded | Lead-gen/performance marketing — no goods, no inventory COGS; different model. |
| GLBE | excluded | High-growth cross-border e-commerce enabler; premium SaaS-like multiple; opposite margin/growth profile. |

## Caveats on the multiples
- HOUR's **gross margin (52.4%) is optically high** because Amazon FBA/fulfillment/shipping and advertising sit in **Selling & Marketing (44% of revenue)**, *below* the gross-profit line, not in COGS. So **EV/Gross-Profit is NOT comparable** to a conventional retailer whose fulfillment sits in COGS — use EV/Sales and EV/EBIT, not EV/GP, for HOUR.
- All peer multiples are **vendor-aggregate (stockanalysis.com)**; per house rule the *subject's* numbers are rebuilt from filings, peers are directional only. EV/EBITDA for the peers is N/M (negative EBITDA), so the only usable cross-company metric is **EV/Sales**.
