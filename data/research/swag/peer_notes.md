# SWAG — Peer Selection Rationale

**Date:** 2026-05-29 · **Subject:** Stran & Company (SWAG), outsourced promotional products / branded merchandise / loyalty (SLS) distributor.

## The comp problem
SWAG is a **sub-scale, asset-light promotional-products distributor** (FY25 rev $116.2M; ~154 employees; it *resells* product sourced from third-party manufacturers/decorators). There is **no clean public micro-cap pure-play** at SWAG's size. The comp set is therefore: (1) the one scaled pure-play (4imprint), (2) an adjacent scaled print/promo platform (Cimpress), and (3) private-market transaction evidence (HALO roll-up, BAMKO deal, sector M&A study) to set the strategic-buyer floor.

## Primary
- **4imprint (FOUR.L) — the anchor.** Identical business model: a direct-marketing distributor of promotional products that captures orders (historically catalog/mail, now mostly online) and drop-ships sourced merchandise. Asset-light, no manufacturing. At scale: $1.35bn revenue, **10.8% operating margin**, ~32% gross margin, net cash, big dividend. Trades **0.7x EV/revenue, ~6.3x EV/EBITDA** (Jun-Jul 2025). This is the single most important comp because it answers the load-bearing question: *what is a profitable, scaled version of Stran worth?* Answer: a real, but unspectacular, ~0.7x revenue / ~6x EBITDA — i.e., the industry is genuinely low-multiple. **This both supports the bull (SWAG at 0.22x is a deep discount even to a low-multiple industry) and disciplines it (the ceiling multiple is not high).**
- **Cimpress (CMPR).** Larger ($3.4bn rev), more vertically integrated (owns print/manufacturing via Vista, National Pen), levered (3.1x net debt/EBITDA). ~12.7% adj-EBITDA margin, **0.9x EV/rev, ~8x EV/EBITDA**. Same end market (branded merchandise/promo/print) but a different (capital-heavier) model, so primary-leaning-secondary.

## Secondary / transaction evidence
- **HALO Branded Solutions (private, TPG-backed).** #2 US distributor, ~$965M North American promo revenue (2024). The relevant read is **strategic-acquirer behavior**: HALO is a serial acquirer of small distributors (multiple tuck-ins in 2024-25), which is exactly the roll-up that Stran is attempting from the *other* (acquiree-adjacent) side. Establishes that small distributors are routinely bought.
- **BAMKO / Superior Group of Companies.** SGC bought BAMKO assets for **$15.8M cash + earnout** on ~$366M North American promo revenue (2024). Promo "revenue" is largely gross merchandise value, so distributors transact on a **multiple of net gross-profit / EBITDA, not gross revenue** — a critical framing for SWAG, whose 0.22x EV/gross-revenue looks artificially cheap but whose EV/gross-profit (~0.78x) and EV/EBITDA are the honest comps.
- **Promo-products M&A composite (Jahani & Associates, 2025).** Most sector deals clear at **1x–2x EV/revenue and 7x–11x EV/EBITDA**; ~90 deals/quarter. Even the *low* end (1x rev / 7x EBITDA) is ~4–5x SWAG's public 0.22x EV/rev — i.e., the private market would not value a comparable book of business at SWAG's public EV.

## Excluded
- **Large ad-holdcos (OMC, IPG, WPP, PUBGY)** — yfinance tags SWAG "Advertising Agencies / Communication Services," but SWAG is a *physical-product distributor*, not a media/creative agency. Different margins (agencies ~15% EBIT on fee revenue), different drivers, different multiples. **This GICS misclassification is itself part of the mispricing diagnosis** (it lands SWAG in the wrong comp screen). Excluded.
- **Vendor-aggregate "EV/EBITDA 75x" (yfinance)** — meaningless: GAAP EBITDA is ~$0.35M because of ~$5.2M public-company overhead and a sub-scale corporate cost base. Use EV/revenue, EV/gross-profit, and segment-EBIT-based comps instead.

## How the comps are used
- The honest valuation anchor is **EV/gross-profit and a normalized segment-EBIT multiple**, not EV/EBITDA (distorted by overhead) or EV/gross-revenue (distorted by GMV accounting).
- Multiples applied are **deliberately below 4imprint's 0.7x rev / 6.3x EBITDA**, because SWAG is sub-scale, unprofitable on a consolidated GAAP basis until Q1'26, carries material-weakness/reaudit governance risk, and has a melting cash balance. The peer set sets the *ceiling*; SWAG's own warts set the discount to it.
