# ARTW — Acquisition / Control-Premium Comps & Entry Assumption

**Purpose:** ground the entry-premium assumption in the carve-out return (MOIC) using **industry-/structure-relevant comps**, not a single deal-specific figure. Created 2026-05-31 in response to "why would there be such a premium on buying?"

**Bottom line:** ARTW is a *controlled-company, no-competing-bidder, friendly* situation. The right comps (controlling-shareholder freeze-outs / negotiated control deals, financial buyer) support **~7–20%**, and a **family-rollover structure supports a near-market (~0%) effective entry** — far below an auction-style take-private premium. This is the single biggest, *legitimately* flexible driver of the presented return (it does not touch any reviewed FV input).

## Why the premium reference class matters here

The pitch is a **friendly, negotiated acquisition of control from the McConnell family (51.5%)** — not a contested take-private of a widely-held company. The relevant academic/market evidence:

| Source | Finding | Relevance to ARTW |
|---|---|---|
| **Subramanian, "Post-Siliconix Freeze-Outs: Theory, Evidence & Policy"** (J. Legal Studies 2007) — [Harvard Olin PDF](http://www.law.harvard.edu/programs/olin_center/papers/pdf/Subramanian_472_revised.pdf) | In 96 controlling-shareholder freeze-outs, **merger-route offers rose +18.1%**, **tender-offer route +7.2%**; **the premium shrinks as the controller's pre-deal stake grows.** | Closest analog: a single controller, no auction. ARTW family stake (51.5%) is large → **low end of the range.** |
| Bargeron et al., **"Why do private acquirers pay so little vs. public acquirers?"** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X08001025)) | Target holders receive **~63% higher premium when the buyer is a public strategic vs. a private/PE buyer.** | Our buyer is a **financial/operator buyer**, not a public strategic → premium-reducing. |
| Practitioner rule of thumb (squeeze-out merger agreements) | Controlling-shareholder squeeze-outs typically priced **~20–30% over market.** | Upper bound for a *controlled-company* deal; still below auction take-privates. |
| **Houlihan Lokey Going-Private Study** ([2024 study PDF](http://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2025/hl-2024-going-private-transaction-study.pdf)) | Median **1-day premium 34.0% (2022) → 46.3% (2023).** | **WRONG reference class** — these are widely-held, often auctioned P2P deals. Cited only to show why borrowing a high figure overstates ARTW's premium. |
| Furey Research (2019), via [Essex](https://www.essexinvest.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Seven-Reasons-to-Invest-in-Micro-Cap.pdf) | Microcap acquisition premiums historically **~2× large-cap.** | Cuts the other way (microcaps can command *more*); a genuine counter-consideration, noted for honesty. |

> **FONR excluded as a reference (v3):** the earlier (v1/v2) anchor borrowed the **31.5% premium** from Fonar Corp's Dec-2025 CEO-led take-private. Fonar is a **diagnostic-imaging (MRI) micro-cap with no overlap** — a name we happened to screen, not an industry/structure comp — and its deal-specific premium is **dropped.** The premium now rests on the structural freeze-out evidence above (Subramanian + the private-buyer discount), which describes *our* situation (single large controller, no auction, financial buyer).

### Reconciling the counter-argument (microcap premiums run high)
Furey's "~2× large cap" is real, but it is an *average across all microcap M&A* — dominated by **strategic buyers in competitive/auction processes**. It does **not** describe a friendly, single-controller, no-auction deal with a financial buyer, which is the ARTW structure. The structure-specific evidence (Subramanian + the private-buyer discount) dominates here.

### Honest reading
- Moving from an **auction-style ~30%** to a **structure-appropriate ~10–15%** lifts **bull MOIC 1.44× → ~1.6–1.7×** and prob-weighted **0.78× → ~0.9×.** A real, comps-grounded improvement to the **upside**.
- A **rollover (near-market) entry** pushes **prob-weighted to ~1.0×** and **bull to ~1.9×.**
- **The base case still does not clear 1.0× at any plausible control-premium entry** — because base intrinsic ($2.46) sits *at/just below* the $2.58 market price (model.md §3, the reviewed finding). The premium fix improves the upside and narrows the base loss; it does **not** manufacture a positive base. The pitch therefore stays an **asymmetry + asset-floor** story, now with a materially better-supported entry.

**Do NOT** re-tune FV inputs (growth/margin/multiple/ag-recovery) to force a positive base — that is the exact error the v2 reviewer caught (model.md §1b). The entry assumption is the only honest lever, and it is well-supported above.
