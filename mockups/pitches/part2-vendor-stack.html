The AI-native sourcing stack

The 2026 tooling for a lean, AI-fluent searcher acquiring a boutique executive-search firm. Four categories (market research, sourcing, CRM, and outbound), selected against three principles.

01Guiding principles

  • One agent runs everything. Every tool is chosen so its data and operations feed a single coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor) that aggregates, processes, and acts on them. The priority is a tool exposing an MCP server or open API the agent can call directly.
  • Differentiated data. When we spend, we prioritize sources that give us a view other buyers don't already have. Commodity data is used at its free tier, or skipped.
  • Fit to the search. Sources are matched to this specific search (executive search, owner-operated firms, demand-driven screening) rather than generic market subscriptions.

02Executive summary

Four layers, each free or cheap, each feeding one agent. Together they cover the funnel from research to logged call for ~$430–800/mo all-in.

Recommendation
Two tools: Superhuman ($33/mo) for email, Handwrytten (~$99/mo) for cards.
  • Superhuman sends from your real domain with read and link tracking, AI drafting, and follow-up reminders.
  • Handwrytten sends a real pen-and-ink card, the channel a founder who ignores email will still open.

04Human vs. AI: who does what

The stack buys leverage, not autonomy. Three principles govern where the agent acts and where a person must, and the balance shifts over time: work starts hybrid and automates further as the library of good examples grows.

1 · AI cannot replace the relationship
Convincing a founder to sell is a high-stakes, trust-dependent sale. Every personalized touch, the emails and notes that go out and every call, is written or edited and sent by a human.
2 · AI accelerates the team
Its job is to focus execution: research, enrichment, drafting, scoring, scheduling, transcription, and pipeline hygiene. That is the volume work that frees the searcher to spend their hours on owners.
3 · AI is only as good as its controls
Output reflects the goals, constraints, and examples we set, so it needs iteration and feedback. Work starts hybrid. As we build a library of what "good" looks like, the agent earns more autonomy on the lower-stakes tasks.
