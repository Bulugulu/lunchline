The AI-native sourcing stack

The 2026 tooling for a lean, AI-fluent searcher acquiring a boutique executive-search firm. Four categories — market research, sourcing, CRM, outbound — selected against three principles.

01Guiding principles

  • One agent runs everything. Every tool is chosen so its data and operations feed a single AI agent that aggregates, processes, and acts on them. The priority is a tool exposing an MCP server or open API the agent can call directly.
  • Differentiated data. We pay only for sources that give a differentiated, alpha-generating view. Commodity data that every buyer already has is used at its free tier, or skipped.
  • Fit to the search. Data sources are matched to this specific search — executive search, owner-operated firms, demand-driven screening — rather than generic, off-the-shelf market subscriptions.

02Executive summary

Four AI-native layers — each free or cheap, each feeding one agent — cover the full funnel from research to logged call for ~$575–950/mo all-in, against $15,000–120,000+/yr per category for the institutional equivalent.

BCompany Research / SourcingJob · build and score the target universe
Recommendation
Clay ($185–495/mo) is the core — orchestration, AI enrichment, and personalization, feeding the agent. Discovery is free: industry directories (AESC, Hunt Scanlon, BlueSteps) plus Google Maps and LinkedIn, scraped and structured by Claygent. The one paid contact tool is Apollo ($79/mo, own-key into Clay) for verified personal emails and email verification, cancelled once the list is enriched. Executive-search firms are web-visible — websites, named partners, LinkedIn, directories — so the universe is self-sourceable for free; a permanent database seat ($10,000–100,000/yr) is not justified. Office phones and addresses come free from websites and Maps; only the verified personal email is worth paying for, mainly to protect email deliverability. What we get per firm: name, site, location, practice/industry mix, partner names, ~headcount, emails, AI-inferred owner and succession flags. What we don't get: audited financials or confirmed sale-intent — those come from inference and the call.

Recommendation

Conversation capture — part of the system of record: every qualifying call and Google Meet transcribed straight into the repo for the agent.

Recommendation
Granola Business ($14/mo) for Google Meet — bot-less, native Windows, transcripts pulled via its MCP server — paired with OpenPhone / Quo Business ($23/mo) for phone calls, which transcribes every call and pushes a webhook to the repo. ~$37/mo. No single bot-less tool captures both Meet on Windows and cell calls, so the two legs split. Both feed one ingestion endpoint (n8n/Zapier) that drops the transcript into the record and triggers the agent to summarize, extract next steps, and update the pipeline. Routing owner calls through the OpenPhone softphone is more reliable than recording a cell call, and it doubles as the phone-outreach channel. Disclose recording at the top of every call (treat as all-party consent).

DOutboundJob · reach owners and earn the reply
Recommendation
Rent the sending infrastructure and build the personalization: Smartlead ($94/mo) for email deliverability, HeyReach ($79/mo) for LinkedIn, Handwrytten (~$99/mo) for pen-and-ink mail; a Claude agent with Clay writes the per-owner message. Deliverability is fragile and worth renting; personalization and orchestration are where the agent adds value. The ≥10% reply target only holds across channels, so LinkedIn and mail carry it, with email as reinforcement. Smartlead's 116-tool MCP and HeyReach's MCP let one agent run all of it.

07One caution that feeds the next deliverable

Reply-rate reality
The ≥10% reply KPI holds only as a multi-channel number. Cold email alone runs ~3.4% across platforms; a searcher who closed a deal reported 2–4% on email. Reaching 10% means leading with LinkedIn (30%+ connection acceptance) and pen-and-ink mail (the highest-signal channel), with email as reinforcement. This sets up the human-vs-automation map: the agent runs the top of the funnel and all logistics, while a human owns every reply and call. Convincing a 60-year-old founder to sell is a trust-dependent sale, and the ETA community is wary of over-automating it.
