ACCESS Newswire Inc.

Thesis

ACCESS Newswire is a 75%-gross-margin, 60%-subscription investor-relations communications-technology business priced at 1.07 times revenue and 19.0 times trailing earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) — multiples set by a peer set of advertising agencies (WPP, Interpublic) that share none of the company's economics. On the correct investor-relations-technology software-as-a-service peer set — Donnelley Financial Solutions (DFIN) at 2.5 times revenue, Q4 Inc. taken private in May 2024 at 7.5 times revenue — fair value is roughly $12 to $14 a share against today's $6.32. The mechanical problem is that a 2.01-million-share float with $87,000 of daily dollar volume cannot absorb institutional buying. This is not a public re-rating; it is a search-fund or private-equity target priced inside the public-market window.

The business

ACCESS Newswire generated $22.5 million of revenue in the twelve months ended March 2026 (per the quarterly report filed May 12, 2026) distributing press releases, hosting investor-relations webcasts, and running a media-monitoring and pitching platform for 12,802 customers across public and private companies. Subscription revenue is 60% of the total at $12.2 million in annual recurring revenue from 974 subscribers as of December 31, 2025, growing to 1,119 subscribers by the end of the first quarter of 2026 (fiscal 2025 annual report, filed March 19, 2026; Q1 2026 earnings call). The founder and chief executive, Brian Balbirnie, has run the company since incorporating it in 1988, owns 16.05% of shares outstanding, and is paid $285,000 a year (proxy statement filed April 30, 2026); three other holders — Topline Capital Management at 18.27%, Yorkmont Capital at 6.89%, and 2717 Partners at 5.44% — bring identified holders above 5% to 46.6% of shares outstanding. The financial fingerprint — 75.6% gross margin, 60% subscription mix, $247,000 of revenue per employee on 91 employees — is identical to investor-relations-technology peers DFIN and the former public-company Q4 Inc., and shares nothing with the advertising-agency peer group the Yahoo Finance tag routes consensus toward.

Why this is a non-obvious pick

By the Lunchline tests, this is mostly under-followed and mispriced on the wrong peer set; it is only mildly messy. Messy: the company divested its Compliance segment to Equiniti for $12.5 million in February 2025 and recorded a $14.15 million impairment on the Newswire trademark in fiscal 2024 (fiscal 2025 10-K, Note on impairment), leaving two years of discontinued-operations footnotes and one large optical loss in the trailing earnings — every screen that filters on auditor flags, discontinued operations, or a five-year clean net-income line excludes it. Mispriced: the Yahoo Finance industry tag is "Advertising Agencies" against a 75.6% gross margin and a 60% recurring-revenue mix that describe an investor-relations-technology software business, not an agency — DFIN trades at 2.5 times revenue (Yahoo Finance quote page); Q4 Inc. was taken private by Sumeru Equity Partners in May 2024 at C$257 million on roughly C$35 million of revenue, or 7.5 times revenue (May 22, 2024 transaction press release); ACCS trades at 1.07 times revenue, roughly 40% of the DFIN benchmark. Under-followed: two analysts publish on the stock (Lake Street and Northland Securities), neither at a bulge-bracket firm, and the public float of 2.01 million shares produces $87,000 of daily dollar volume — below the threshold at which any fund managing more than $25 million in deployable capital can build a position without moving the price several percent on entry.

What the market sees

Two analysts cover the stock. Lake Street's Jacob Stephan initiated coverage on April 24, 2025 at a $15 price target, cut to $14 in August 2025, and cut again to $12 on May 13, 2026 — three downward revisions in thirteen months, with the next-fiscal-year earnings-per-share estimate cut from $0.80 to $0.44 over the same window. Northland Securities' Mike Grondahl publishes more sporadically and was the only sell-side voice on the fourth-quarter 2025 call. The stock trades at 1.07 times trailing revenue and 19.0 times trailing GAAP EBITDA (Yahoo Finance key statistics) — both numbers essentially unchanged from a year earlier despite the divestiture of Compliance and the rebrand to ACCESS Newswire, and against a 36% twelve-month price decline. The implication priced in: a sub-scale newswire competitor in a "fragmented and extremely competitive" industry (the 10-K's own phrase) that is shrinking in volume terms, with a trademark impairment in the recent past and a sell-side that keeps lowering the target.

What we see

The income statement the market is grading was produced by a public-company cost structure that is too small to amortize and a sector tag that routes valuation to the wrong peer set. First, the gross margin is 75.6% on a 60%-subscription revenue mix — the economics of a software business, not the 20%-to-25%-gross-margin services economics of the WPP and Interpublic peer group implied by the "Advertising Agencies" classification. Second, fiscal 2025 GAAP EBITDA of $1.25 million is suppressed by roughly $1.5 million of public-company overhead — Securities and Exchange Commission compliance, listing fees, audit, directors-and-officers insurance, and the cost of running an investor-relations function for a $24 million-enterprise-value company — and by $864,000 of disclosed non-recurring expense; the adjusted EBITDA management reported for fiscal 2025 was $3.2 million (FY25 10-K, management discussion). Applying the DFIN multiple of 2.5 times revenue to today's $22.5 million revenue yields $56 million of enterprise value, or roughly $14.50 a share after the $0.5 million of net cash; applying the Q4 Inc. take-private multiple of 7.5 times revenue yields a number that is not relevant here because no public buyer would pay it. The honest number sits inside the DFIN-range — call it $11 to $14.

But the float of 2.01 million shares against $87,000 of daily dollar volume is the mechanical problem. The four identified holders above 5% own 46.6% of shares outstanding (proxy statement, April 30, 2026), and none of them — Balbirnie, Topline Capital, Yorkmont, 2717 Partners — has registered shares for sale. Topline's 18.27% position represents roughly seven months of average trading volume at today's pace. A fund that wanted to build even a $1 million position would move the price several percent on entry and could not exit at all without a block trade. The right multiple cannot be expressed in the public stock price because the public stock price is set by retail order flow and a two-analyst Lake Street/Northland coverage seat. The unlock is a corporate action — either a take-private at a $9 to $10 price (mechanically feasible: the four top holders plus an arms-length premium tender clears 90%) or a sponsor-funded recapitalization that uses ACCS as a roll-up platform for the dispersed long-tail of newswire competitors. This is the search-fund framing the title implies; it is not a public re-rate.

What management says versus what the filings disclose

On the May 12, 2026 first-quarter call, Balbirnie said the company is "neck and neck, literally like 0.1% with Business Wire and us in the third position" by U.S. newswire volume and that "Globe and PR Newswire still are the top 1 and 2 in that space" (Q1 2026 earnings call transcript, internal copy). The fiscal 2025 10-K says only that "the communications industry remains both fragmented and extremely competitive" and names no competitor (Item 1, Business). The IR framing of "#3" understates that the third-place slot is contested with a Berkshire Hathaway-owned incumbent — Business Wire — that has effectively unlimited capacity to defend share. On margin guidance, Balbirnie told the fourth-quarter 2025 call he expected "adjusted EBITDA margins into the mid- to high-teens by the second half of this year"; first-quarter 2026 actual adjusted EBITDA was $564,000, or 11% of revenue, down sequentially from 15% in the fourth quarter — the bridge to a mid-teens margin requires a 600-basis-point step-up in two quarters from a declining base. And on artificial-intelligence positioning, the 10-K states that the company's ACCESS Verified verification tool, described by Balbirnie on the Q1 2026 call as a "competitive differentiator that not any other wire service can match in this depth," is "available at no additional cost to customers" — meaning it is a defensive retention feature, not a revenue lever. None of these is a misrepresentation; each cuts the same way — IR-aspirational, filings-cautious — and a careful reader gets a meaningfully different picture from the 10-K than from the call.

What an operator-investor would do

Commercial

Convert the 11,683 transactional (pay-per-release) customers — the 12,802 active accounts less the 1,119 current subscribers per the Q1 2026 call — to subscription at the existing $12,800 average annual recurring revenue per subscriber. A 10% conversion equals 1,170 new subscribers and roughly $11.7 million of additional recurring revenue at 75% gross margin, against $1 of incremental sales cost per dollar of conversion. Comparable: Meltwater's 2020-to-2023 pivot from pay-per-use to subscription, taking gross margin from 73% to 82% before going private at a 95% subscription mix.

Operations

Close the gap between the current 84% operating-expense-to-revenue ratio and the 60% to 65% level investor-relations-technology peers carry at scale. The Raleigh headquarters lease was sublet in December 2025 for $320,000 of annual savings; closing the remaining 1,500 basis points of operating-expense leverage on a $22 million revenue base equals roughly $3.3 million of additional EBITDA. Comparable: DFIN's adjusted-EBITDA margin moved from 14% to 22% over 2016 to 2023 after the spin from RR Donnelley, executed without revenue growth.

Capital structure

Take the company private at $9 to $10 a share (a 40% to 60% premium to today's $6.32) to eliminate roughly $1.5 million of public-company overhead — closing the gap between $1.25 million of GAAP EBITDA and the implied software-business margin — and to clear the float problem that prevents any public-market re-rating. The four holders above 5% own 46.6% of shares; a tender at this premium plus the existing $837,000 unfilled buyback gets a sponsor over 90% mechanically. Comparable: Q4 Inc. taken private by Sumeru Equity Partners in May 2024 at 7.5 times revenue, a transaction that explicitly removed public-company costs from a sub-scale investor-relations-technology software business.

Mergers and acquisitions

Use the ACCS distribution backbone as a roll-up platform for the dispersed long-tail of newswire competitors created by the Cision / PR Newswire ownership churn — Cision taken private by Platinum Equity in January 2020, Notified spun out in 2024 — and the January 2025 Diamond Comic Distributors bankruptcy in adjacent distribution. Five $5 million-revenue targets at 0.6 times revenue equals $15 million of consideration absorbed onto the fixed editorial and distribution stack at 70%-plus incremental gross margin. The $14.15 million Newswire trademark impairment in fiscal 2024 is the cautionary mark: the first roll-up move (Newswire.com, 2022) was overpaid. The next campaign needs sponsor balance sheet — ACCS's $1.5 million revolver and $3 million of cash cannot fund it.

The Operations and Commercial moves are already under way at management's pace. The Capital Structure move is the one that resolves the mispricing.

What would have to go wrong

  1. If first-half 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin does not step up from the 11% reported for Q1 to the mid-teens management guided on the Q4 2025 call, the $3.2 million adjusted EBITDA base disappears and the base case ($4.7 million after public-co-cost removal) becomes $3 million — the take-private math gets thinner, not impossible, but the buyer pool shrinks.
  2. If revenue declines another 5% or more in fiscal 2026 (against down 2% in fiscal 2025 and down 6% in fiscal 2024 per the 10-K), the "shrinking pie" framing becomes the dominant narrative and the comparison to DFIN — which is growing — stops holding. The 10-K admits the industry data underpinning the $5.5 to $6.0 billion total-addressable-market figure is from "early 2023" Burton-Taylor data; the underlying market may already be smaller.
  3. If none of the four identified holders above 5% engages with a take-private process — Topline, Yorkmont, and 2717 Partners are activist-style holders who could either initiate or block — the float problem cannot be solved by a third-party sponsor alone, and the stock remains a "fair value cannot be expressed in the public price" name indefinitely. Lake Street has cut the price target from $15 to $14 to $12 in thirteen months; without a corporate action, the path of least resistance is another cut.

What would confirm it

Three observations over the next four quarterly filings would, taken together, support the case — and one is a red line. First, second-quarter 2026 adjusted EBITDA prints at $750,000 or above (a 13%-plus margin), confirming the bridge management described on the fourth-quarter 2025 call to a mid-teens second-half margin. Second, a Schedule 13D filing — from Topline, from a new sponsor, or from a financial-buyer consortium — naming a corporate action; absent a 13D, the structural-discount thesis remains academic. Third, fiscal 2026 revenue declines no more than 2%, holding the floor under the DFIN-multiple comparison; a steeper decline forces a different peer set. The honest framing for this name is that it is a search-fund or private-equity target whose fair value sits roughly $4 to $7 above where it trades, but where the resolution mechanism runs through an event we cannot observe from the outside. Whether to hold while waiting — given Lake Street's downward path and the absence of catalyst on the calendar — is a judgment the reader can make from the same filings we did.
